# Kleari 0.1.0-beta.4

> **Installing for the first time, or on a new Mac?** Read
> [Read me before you install](../install.md#read-me-before-you-install) first. macOS warns you
> the first time you open Kleari: choose **Done** (never **Move to Trash**), then **Open
> Anyway** in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

The fourth beta of Kleari: remove things right where you find them, your Trash inside Kleari,
pictures and one filter bar on every list, a search that finds everything Kleari has already
looked at, a keyboard cleaning mode, and a new identifier on your Mac. Kleari is free,
open-source software provided as is, and it only acts on what you approve. Everything happens
on your Mac: no account, no telemetry, and the only thing that goes online is the update
check.

**Requires** a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later.

## Install or update

If you have an earlier beta, Kleari offers this update in Settings › About (or when it opens).
Otherwise download `Kleari_0.1.0-beta.4_aarch64.dmg` below, open it and drag Kleari into
Applications; the disk image window now shows the first-open steps too.

## After updating: allow Kleari's permissions again

This beta gives Kleari a new identifier on your Mac, `dev.kleari.app` (it was
`dev.bakare.kleari`). At the first launch, Kleari moves your history, settings and quarantine
to the new name on its own, and macOS forgets the permissions you gave the old one. A
one-time window lists the ones you used (Accessibility for the Mouse settings, Full Disk
Access, notifications, Open at login), each with a button that opens the right place in
System Settings. More in [After updating from beta 3 or older](../install.md#after-updating-from-beta-3-or-older).

## What's new

- **Remove items where you see them.** Apps, Files, Storage, Developer and login items no
  longer send things to Cleanup's review. One confirm sheet lists what will go, with icons and
  Show in Finder, and lets you **Delete permanently** or **Move to quarantine** (with Undo).
  Removing an app offers what it left behind straight after.
- **Your Trash, in Kleari:** a Trash tab on the Quarantine page lists your Trash and each
  drive's, with where each item came from. Put Back, Delete permanently or Empty Trash. Needs
  Full Disk Access.
- **App caches:** clear an app's caches from Apps, or from Maintenance's new App caches tab,
  sorted by size. Never Apple's apps, never while the app is open, never its settings or data.
- **More AI clutter:** throwaway parts of Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI (sessions, logs,
  temporary files; never their settings or memory), and what AI tools left in `/tmp`.
- **Pictures:** thumbnails and a large preview in Duplicates, Similar Images, Large & Old,
  Downloads, Storage Browse and the Trash, and real app icons in Apps, Browser care and
  Maintenance.
- **A filter bar on every list:** sort by size, date or name, filter by type and search by
  name. Each list remembers how you sorted and filtered it.
- **Dashboard search** finds anything Kleari has already looked at since it opened (pages,
  settings, scan results, quarantine, processes, the activity log), grouped by page. It
  never starts a scan.
- **Clean the keyboard:** from Maintenance or the menu-bar panel, Kleari locks the keyboard
  for 30 seconds to 5 minutes so you can wipe it. Any click unlocks it. Needs Accessibility.
- **Mouse:** many new button actions (Show Desktop, Spotlight, switching apps, windows and
  tabs, screenshots, tiling, Lock Mac and more), shortcuts like ⌘⇥ can be recorded, and the
  Mouse page lists your connected mice.
- **Files › Downloads:** open folders in the results and keep going deeper.
- **Storage:** Reveal in Finder for a large file.
- **Report a problem** (Settings › About › Help) opens a GitHub issue filled in with Kleari's
  version, your macOS version and chip kind, and shows Kleari's local log in Finder so you can
  choose to attach it. The log never holds keys, file names, paths, window titles or
  addresses, and nothing is sent unless you submit the issue.

## Fixed and changed

- **Apps** finds apps in folders inside Applications (such as DaVinci Resolve's or Cisco's),
  and lists macOS's own apps as part of macOS, never removed. Xcode, Logic Pro and other Apple
  apps from the App Store can now be removed. A rescan keeps the page usable and keeps your
  choice, and an app removed during a scan no longer comes back.
- Leftovers that a running app still uses are never offered.
- **Uninstall** asks macOS to forget Kleari's permissions itself, and gives you the command
  only if that failed.
- Opening Kleari while it runs brings it forward instead of starting a second copy.
- The menu-bar panel sizes itself to what it shows.
- Mouse leaves trackpad clicks alone.
- Files' type filters work in every group; Downloads' untagged items say "Saved or copied
  here" instead of "Unknown".
- Similar Images is much faster on large and RAW photos.
- Buttons say what they do in full, and dialogs fit inside the window.
- Kleari's window now blocks every outside connection except the update check, with a CI
  check and a list of every address it contacts in
  [network.md](../network.md).
- Settings › About now has the disclaimer, privacy summary, licence and third-party notices,
  and a short "use at your own risk" note is shown once.
- **Removed:** the Mouse actions Do nothing, Middle click and the media keys (a button set to
  one goes back to its usual click), and Maintenance's "Not offered" tab.

The full list is in the [changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md).

## Known limitations

- Kleari isn't notarised by Apple, so macOS asks you to allow it the first time.
- Because of the new identifier, macOS asks for Kleari's permissions again once after this
  update. If an old "Kleari" entry stays in System Settings › Privacy & Security, remove it
  with the − button.
- The Trash tab and Put Back need Full Disk Access.
- Caches of editors kept in Application Support (such as Cursor's) aren't offered: Kleari
  never touches Application Support for app caches.
- The processor's temperature and the fans are read through interfaces Apple doesn't
  document; a macOS update could stop them working.
- Alert thresholds are fixed, and Kleari can't tell when an app has stopped responding.
