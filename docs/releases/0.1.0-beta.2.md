# Kleari 0.1.0-beta.2

> **Installing for the first time, or on a new Mac?** Read
> [Read me before you install](../install.md#read-me-before-you-install) first. macOS warns you
> the first time you open Kleari: choose **Done** (never **Move to Trash**), then **Open
> Anyway** in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

The second beta of Kleari, with new pages for apps, files, maintenance and browsers, issue
alerts, and fixes to the first beta. Everything still happens on your Mac only: no account,
no telemetry, nothing sent.

**Requires** a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later.

## Install or update

If you have beta 1, Kleari offers this update in Settings › About (or when it opens). Otherwise
download `Kleari_0.1.0-beta.2_aarch64.dmg` below, open it and drag Kleari into Applications.

When it first opens, Kleari offers three optional things: **Full Disk Access**, **Open at
login** and **Show in the menu bar**, with the steps for each. You can change them later in
Settings.

## What's new

- **Apps:** see what each app keeps in your Library, and remove an app completely: it goes to
  the Trash (Finder's Put Back still works), and what it left behind goes to Cleanup's review.
  Also finds data left by apps you already deleted, and installers you've already used.
- **Files:** Large & Old files, exact Duplicates (one copy is always kept), Similar Images,
  Downloads by the app that downloaded them, and AI clutter (model downloads, AI apps' caches
  and logs).
- **Storage › Browse:** open any folder in your home folder down to single files, with a map
  of what takes the most space, and choose what to clear.
- **Maintenance:** free up memory, flush the DNS cache, free up purgeable space, reindex
  Spotlight, clear system caches and old logs, remove deleted users and empty the Trash. Most
  ask for your password through macOS's own prompt; Kleari never sees it. Also lists login and
  background items.
- **Browser care:** how hard Safari, Chrome, Brave and Edge are working, and their tabs, so you
  can close the ones you don't need.
- **Issue alerts** on the Dashboard (and as notifications if you turn them on): a nearly full
  disk or drive, memory pressure, fast battery drain, a Bluetooth device's low battery, a large
  Trash and apps you haven't opened in months.
- **Cleanup** also finds damaged preferences files and login items whose program is gone, and
  Xcode's previews.
- **Mouse:** switch apps with a held button and the wheel (like ⌘⇥), and step through an app's
  windows.
- **Step-by-step guides** wherever Kleari asks for a permission.

What you choose to clear still goes through Cleanup's review and preview, and moves only into
quarantine, where you can restore it. Removed apps go to the Trash; Maintenance tasks that
delete say so and ask you to confirm.

## Fixed and changed

- The menu-bar panel showed an old name and appeared without its styling.
- A project counts as active (and isn't suggested) only while one of its files is open in an
  app, not because of a recent commit.
- With hidden files off, build folders such as `.next` are still searched; only private files
  such as `.env` are skipped.
- Large and old files say what kind of file they are.
- The Activity page is no longer blank while it loads.

The full list is in the [changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md).

## Known limitations

- Kleari isn't notarised by Apple, so macOS asks you to allow it the first time.
- The first time you list a browser's tabs or empty the Trash, macOS asks whether Kleari may
  control that app; removing an app may need App Management allowed.
- Leftovers of deleted apps are found by their names, so some may be missed; check each one
  before you approve it.
- Copies made by Finder's Duplicate can share space on disk, so removing one may free less than
  its size.
- Kleari can't tell when an app has stopped responding: macOS doesn't let apps find out.
- Apple's Magic Mouse scrolls like a trackpad, so it keeps the trackpad's direction, and mouse
  settings work only while Kleari is running, with Accessibility allowed.
