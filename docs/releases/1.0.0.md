# Kleari 1.0.0

> **Installing for the first time, or on a new Mac?** Read
> [Read me before you install](../install.md#read-me-before-you-install) first. macOS warns you
> the first time you open Kleari: choose **Done** (never **Move to Trash**), then **Open
> Anyway** in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

Kleari 1.0.0 is the first stable release. It keeps your Mac clean and fast, on your Mac only:
it finds space you can reclaim, moves it to a quarantine you can undo, shows what your Mac is
doing, tidies developer caches and makes a mouse work your way. Kleari is free, open-source
software provided as is, and it only acts on what you approve. Everything happens on your
Mac: no account, no telemetry, and the only thing that goes online is the update check.

**Requires** a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later.

## Install or update

Download `Kleari_1.0.0_aarch64.dmg` below, open it and drag Kleari into Applications.

If you have a beta, Kleari offers this update in Settings › About (or when it opens). Coming
from beta 4, 5 or 6, you keep your permissions. Coming from beta 3 or older, see
[After updating from beta 3 or older](../install.md#after-updating-from-beta-3-or-older).

**Beta testers:** if you never changed **Beta updates** in Settings › About, it followed the
beta you had installed. Once you're on 1.0.0 it's off, and Kleari offers stable releases only.
Turn it on to keep getting betas.

## What's in it

- **Dashboard:** storage, memory, CPU and battery at a glance, your last scan and what
  quarantine holds, with search across everything Kleari has already looked at.
- **Cleanup:** scans for caches, logs, temporary files, package caches and developer build
  folders, explains each finding and its risk, and moves only what you approve into
  quarantine. Optional scheduled scans only read, then notify you.
- **Quarantine:** restore anything, never overwriting; delete permanently only with a second
  confirmation. A Trash tab shows your Trash, with Put Back.
- **Storage:** how your startup disk is used, by folder and category, and files of 1 GB or more.
- **System:** live CPU, memory, swap, battery, thermal state, network and disk activity, this
  Mac's details and its startup items.
- **Processes:** what is running, with a polite, confirmed Quit for regular apps.
- **Developer:** Xcode, simulators, npm, Homebrew, Cargo and other caches, project build
  folders and Docker's disk use (read-only).
- **Mouse:** for an external mouse, a wheel that scrolls the classic way while the trackpad
  stays natural, wheel speed and smooth scrolling, and buttons and combinations for tabs,
  windows, desktops, screenshots and your own shortcuts.
- **Menu bar:** live readings and your last scan, one click away.
- **Activity:** a local log of what Kleari did.
- **Updates:** signed updates from Kleari's GitHub releases, installed only when you choose.

## Since beta 6

Nothing but the version. Coming from beta 5 or older, Install and restart can still show a
second Kleari icon in the Dock once, because the version you update from does the restart.
Quit Kleari and open it from the Dock to clear it.

The full history is in the [changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md).

## Known limitations

- Kleari isn't notarised by Apple, so macOS asks you to allow it the first time.
- The Trash tab and Put Back need Full Disk Access.
- Caches of editors kept in Application Support (such as Cursor's) aren't offered: Kleari
  never touches Application Support for app caches.
- The processor's temperature and the fans are read through interfaces Apple doesn't
  document; a macOS update could stop them working.
- Alert thresholds are fixed, and Kleari can't tell when an app has stopped responding.
